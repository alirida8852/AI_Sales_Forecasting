## DOC-001 — SalesSignal_Sales_Playbook_v1.0.md

- Document type: Sales Playbook
- Access scope: internal

### CHUNK-001-A: Sales process overview

SalesSignal uses a consultative sales process with five stages: Lead Captured, Qualified, Discovery Complete, Demo Completed, and Proposal Sent. A deal becomes Closed Won only after the customer signs the order form and payment terms are accepted. A deal becomes Closed Lost when the prospect declines, selects another vendor, or becomes unresponsive after three documented follow-ups over at least 15 business days.

### CHUNK-001-B: Lead qualification criteria

A lead is Qualified when the sales representative has confirmed a relevant business problem, an identified decision-maker or buying sponsor, a plausible budget, and an expected decision timeline. Record company size, industry, current CRM, primary use case, and next step in SalesSignal. If budget or authority is unknown, keep the lead in Discovery rather than marking it Qualified.

### CHUNK-001-C: Follow-up and next steps

After a qualified lead is confirmed, the account owner should schedule a product demonstration within five business days. After every customer interaction, update the CRM activity log and assign a dated next step. If a prospect does not reply, use up to three follow-ups across at least 15 business days before marking the opportunity Unresponsive. Do not promise features or delivery dates that are not listed in the approved product roadmap.

## DOC-002 — SalesSignal_Product_Guide_v1.2.md

- Document type: Product Guide
- Access scope: internal

### CHUNK-002-A: Product overview

SalesSignal is a fictional AI-assisted customer relationship management platform for small and mid-sized sales teams. It centralizes lead records, account activity, opportunity stages, follow-up reminders, and pipeline reporting. Its AI features provide lead-priority suggestions and summaries of recorded interactions; sales representatives remain responsible for validating recommendations.

### CHUNK-002-B: Core features

SalesSignal includes lead scoring, configurable sales pipelines, task reminders, activity history, team dashboards, CSV import and export, and integrations with supported email and calendar tools. Lead scores are prioritization aids, not guarantees of conversion. Users should review the underlying lead details before deciding whom to contact.

### CHUNK-002-C: Product limitations

SalesSignal does not independently verify the accuracy of imported CRM records, guarantee revenue outcomes, or make binding pricing and contract decisions. Integrations depend on the customer's supported provider and configuration. Custom integrations require technical review and may involve additional fees. Do not describe an unreleased roadmap item as an available feature.

## DOC-003 — SalesSignal_Pricing_and_Discounts_v2.0.md

- Document type: Pricing Policy
- Access scope: internal

### CHUNK-003-A: Subscription tiers

SalesSignal has three fictional subscription tiers. Starter costs USD 29 per user per month and includes core CRM records, a standard pipeline, reminders, and basic reporting. Growth costs USD 49 per user per month and adds AI lead-priority suggestions, advanced pipeline analytics, and supported email and calendar integrations. Enterprise uses custom pricing and requires a written quote approved by the Sales Manager.

### CHUNK-003-B: Billing and plan changes

Published Starter and Growth prices are per user per month when billed monthly. Any annual-billing offer must be shown on a current approved quote; staff must not invent or estimate an annual discount. Plan changes take effect at the next billing cycle unless the order form explicitly states otherwise. Enterprise pricing, implementation fees, and custom integration fees must be documented in the signed order form.

### CHUNK-003-C: Discount approval rules

SalesSignal's standard maximum discount is 10% for Starter and Growth subscriptions, subject to the account owner's approval and recorded business justification. Discounts greater than 10% and up to 20% require written Sales Manager approval and are limited to deals with a first-year subscription value above USD 50,000. Discounts above 20% require executive approval and must not be promised before approval is recorded. Never apply a discount retroactively without Finance approval.

## DOC-004 — SalesSignal_Objection_Handling_v1.1.md

- Document type: Sales Enablement
- Access scope: internal

### CHUNK-004-A: Objection: too expensive

When a prospect says SalesSignal is too expensive, ask which business outcome matters most and connect the relevant approved feature to that outcome. Discuss the cost of manual follow-up or limited pipeline visibility only when the prospect has confirmed those problems. Do not guarantee a specific return on investment or promise unapproved discounts. Refer to SalesSignal_Pricing_and_Discounts_v2.0.md for discount approval limits.

### CHUNK-004-B: Objection: already using a CRM

When a prospect already uses another CRM, ask what works well and what remains difficult. Explain SalesSignal's relevant capabilities, such as lead-priority suggestions and pipeline analytics, without claiming that every competitor lacks those features. Confirm integration requirements before promising migration or compatibility. Offer a demo focused on the prospect's stated workflow.

### CHUNK-004-C: Objection: not ready to decide

When a prospect needs more time, ask what information or stakeholder input is missing. Agree on a specific follow-up date and share only approved product or pricing materials. A pilot may be discussed only if the current offer and eligibility are approved by the Sales Manager. Do not create artificial deadlines or claim that an offer expires unless the written offer states that date.

## DOC-005 — SalesSignal_Customer_Onboarding_v1.0.md

- Document type: Customer Success
- Access scope: internal

### CHUNK-005-A: Onboarding timeline

After a deal is marked Closed Won and the order form is complete, Customer Success should contact the customer within two business days and target onboarding completion within seven calendar days. The onboarding owner records the kickoff date, customer administrator, agreed use cases, data import needs, training attendees, and outstanding blockers in SalesSignal.

### CHUNK-005-B: Onboarding responsibilities

The customer administrator provides authorized user details and validates imported records. SalesSignal Customer Success coordinates workspace setup, standard CSV import guidance, administrator training, and a review of the customer's sales pipeline stages. Custom data cleanup or bespoke integration work is not included in standard onboarding unless it is explicitly listed in the signed order form.

### CHUNK-005-C: Post-onboarding check-in

The Customer Success Manager schedules a check-in approximately 30 days after onboarding completion. Review active users, lead-record completeness, use of reminders, pipeline-stage adoption, and reported blockers. Log agreed actions with an owner and due date. Escalate unresolved product defects through the internal support process rather than promising an unconfirmed fix date.

## DOC-006 — SalesSignal_Support_and_Escalation_v1.0.md

- Document type: Support Policy
- Access scope: internal

### CHUNK-006-A: Support severity levels

SalesSignal support classifies a Severity 1 incident as a confirmed widespread outage or a critical security incident affecting production. Severity 2 means a major feature is unavailable for multiple users with no reasonable workaround. Severity 3 covers limited-impact defects or issues with a workaround. Severity 4 covers general questions and cosmetic issues. Support staff assign severity based on impact and evidence, not customer seniority alone.

### CHUNK-006-B: Escalation and communication

Severity 1 incidents must be escalated immediately to the on-call engineering lead and Support Manager. Severity 2 incidents are routed to the Support Manager during the same business day. For every escalation, record the affected workspace, observed behavior, start time, business impact, reproduction steps, and relevant logs with sensitive data removed. Communicate confirmed facts and the next update time; do not speculate about root cause.

### CHUNK-006-C: Support scope

Standard support covers product usage questions, troubleshooting of supported features, and investigation of reproducible defects. It does not include custom development, third-party provider support, or guaranteed resolution times unless a signed contract states otherwise. Never ask customers to send passwords, secret keys, or full payment-card details in a support ticket.

## DOC-008 — SalesSignal_API_Documentation_v1.0.md

- Document type: Technical Documentation
- Access scope: partners

### CHUNK-008-A: API overview

SalesSignal provides a fictional REST API for authorized integrations to read lead records, update permitted opportunity fields, and retrieve pipeline summaries. API access is available only to approved workspaces and integrations. The API documentation in this demo dataset is illustrative and does not describe a live production endpoint.

### CHUNK-008-B: Authentication and permissions

The illustrative SalesSignal API uses bearer-token authentication. Tokens must be stored in an approved secret manager and must not be committed to source control or included in logs. Integrations should request only the permissions required for their function. Revoke and rotate a token if exposure is suspected. This fictional document does not provide real credentials or a working authentication endpoint.

### CHUNK-008-C: Example integration behavior

A partner integration may retrieve a lead by its identifier, update an allowed next-step field, and record the result in an audit log. Requests should handle authorization failures, validation errors, rate limits, and temporary server errors. Retry transient failures with bounded exponential backoff. Validate all fields before writing changes. This is conceptual guidance for testing RAG retrieval, not executable API documentation.

## DOC-009 — SalesSignal_Data_Access_and_Retention_v1.0.md

- Document type: Data Governance Policy
- Access scope: internal

### CHUNK-009-A: Access scope definitions

SalesSignal knowledge documents use three access scopes in this demo dataset. Internal documents are available only to authenticated employees with the relevant role. Public documents may be retrieved by any authorized application user. Partner documents are available only to approved partner identities. A RAG application must enforce access scope in retrieval filters before sending chunks to the language model; metadata labels alone do not enforce security.

### CHUNK-009-B: Data minimization and sensitive data

Store only information required for a defined business purpose. Do not add real customer secrets, passwords, payment-card data, or unnecessary personal information to the knowledge index. Remove or redact sensitive values before embedding or logging. Access checks must be applied to every retrieval request, including follow-up questions and cached results.

### CHUNK-009-C: Retention and deletion

For this fictional demo, customer workspace records are retained for 90 days after subscription cancellation unless a signed contract or applicable legal requirement specifies a different period. Deletion requests must be recorded and routed to the authorized data administrator. When indexed source content is deleted or replaced, remove or refresh its associated chunks and embeddings so stale content is not returned.

## DOC-010 — SalesSignal_CRM_Data_Quality_v1.0.md

- Document type: CRM Operations Guide
- Access scope: internal

### CHUNK-010-A: Required lead fields

Every active lead record should include lead name or organization, source, owner, lifecycle stage, industry when known, last activity date, and next action. A Qualified lead should additionally include the confirmed business problem, buying contact or sponsor, budget status, and expected decision timeline. If a field is unknown, mark it Unknown rather than inventing a value.

### CHUNK-010-B: Duplicate records and updates

Before creating a new lead, search for an existing record using email address and organization name. If a likely duplicate exists, verify it before merging. Preserve the most complete activity history and record the merge in the audit trail. Update opportunity stage only when the stage criteria have been met and the supporting customer interaction is logged.

### CHUNK-010-C: Forecasting rules

SalesSignal pipeline forecasts use opportunity amount multiplied by the stage probability configured by the sales operations administrator. Forecasts are estimates, not guaranteed revenue. Do not manually inflate stage probability to reach a target. Review close date, amount, stage, and next action weekly; flag opportunities with no activity for 14 days for owner review.
